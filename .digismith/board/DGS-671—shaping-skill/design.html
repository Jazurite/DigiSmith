What and why

A ticket is created in backlog as a raw idea. A new skill, digismith:shaping, runs on it. Only after that does the ticket reach concept. So concept now means shaped, no longer being shaped (DGS-488). Claiming, by the factory or by hand, is allowed only after concept (DGS-670).

The method is Basecamp's Shape Up: a pitch with problem, appetite, solution, rabbit holes and no-gos, made in four steps.

The flow

It follows the four Shape Up steps. The skill writes no code, makes no orders and starts no workers (Shape Up's "two tracks": shaping is separate from building).

  1. Read. dg clickup get-task. The ticket must be in backlog, or in concept without a pitch.
  2. Set boundaries. Fix the appetite first ("fixed time, variable scope": if the solution does not fit, cut scope, never raise the appetite). Narrow the problem: ask what is really going wrong and where today's workflow breaks. A grab-bag ("redesign X", "X 2.0") is reframed or split before shaping. A Big appetite that cannot be narrowed means split.
  3. Rough out the elements. Draft the solution at "fat marker" level: the main parts and how they connect, with the fine details left open.
  4. Address rabbit holes. Find unknowns that could trip the builder. For each, amend, cut, or specify it. Write the no-gos. Propose Sign-off and Mode with one reason each.
  5. Ask. At most three questions to Jack, only on what the draft could not settle.
  6. Write the pitch. Show it to Jack. On his yes, append it to the ticket description under ## Pitch (the raw idea above it stays untouched), then run dg steward event shaped --ticket <KEY> --by shaping.
  7. Hand over. Tell Jack the next step: "discover it" or "refine it".
Walk away is a valid end. Shaping can end in "not now". The ticket stays in backlog with a one-line note on why, and no event fires. Jack decides whether to discard it. The skill never discards.

The pitch

  1. Problem: one concrete story of why things do not work today.
  2. Appetite: Small, Medium or Big. A budget that limits the solution, not an estimate.
  3. Solution: the core elements, rough.
  4. Rabbit holes: what could derail the work, each with the call made on it.
  5. No-gos: what is explicitly out.
  6. Sign-off and Mode: proposed values with a reason each (DigiSmith addition; Jack confirms them when he approves into to do).
  7. Open questions: what shaping could not settle. A pitch with open questions can still ship, but they are listed.

Refinement reads this pitch as its input, so digismith:brainstorming no longer asks again for problem and scope.

The Steward event

shaped moves a ticket from backlog to concept. The skill fires it as its last step, after Jack says the pitch is fine. It needs no quote: it is not one of the two gates (refinement to to do, and acceptance).

  • concept-started is removed. Nothing would fire it any more, and keeping it would let a ticket enter concept without shaping.
  • discovery-started and refinement-started are unchanged and still start from concept.
  • dg steward event stays a state machine: a fixed map of status moves. The change is one row in the rules table and its tests.

Neighbours and old tickets

  • digismith:brainstorming stays the refinement step (architecture, then Jack approves the spec). It starts from the pitch.
  • digismith:jira-intake is untouched. It is the Jira door. Shaping is the ClickUp door.
  • Tickets already in concept (DGS-625, 626, 633, 651 and others) stay there. They were moved before any shaping. The first refiner or claimer who touches one runs the skill on it. No mass move.

Overlap with brainstorming

Compared with digismith:brainstorming (the DigiSmith fork of the Superpowers skill). Shaping overlaps it in four rules and differs in four.

  • Brainstorming becomes specific. The original is an umbrella ("use before any creative work"). In DigiSmith the work is split by role: shaping owns the product level (what, why, appetite, scope, no-gos), brainstorming owns the technical level (architecture, components, data flow, errors, testing, Jack approves the spec). Its description changes to "architecture and solution design for a ticket in refinement" and drops the umbrella trigger. It keeps its name: init, bootstrap, writing-plans and the DGS-488 text refer to it, and a rename would only add churn.
  • The pitch is its input. In refinement brainstorming reads the pitch as given and does not ask again about purpose, scope, appetite or success criteria. A ticket in refinement with no pitch goes back to shaping first.
  • No double trigger. With the umbrella trigger gone, a raw idea no longer matches brainstorming. A backlog ticket goes to digismith:shaping, which never invokes brainstorming.
  • No copies. skills/shaping/SKILL.md says "conversation rules as in digismith:brainstorming" (one question at a time, multiple choice, decomposition) and spells out only what differs: the pitch, the appetite, the event.
