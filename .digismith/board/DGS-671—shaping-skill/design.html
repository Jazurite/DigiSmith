What and why

A ticket is created in backlog as a raw idea. A new skill, digismith:shaping, runs on it. Only after that does the ticket reach concept. So concept now means shaped, no longer being shaped (DGS-488). Claiming, by the factory or by hand, is allowed only after concept (DGS-670).

Shape Up is a methodology, not a spec. We take the parts that fit DigiSmith and adapt them to our status model, and we leave the rest (section "Shape Up: where we follow it and where we differ"). The ideas taken are from Basecamp's Shape Up: a pitch made from boundaries (appetite, a narrowed problem) and a rough idea. Shape Up's solution-design steps (elements, rabbit holes) go to refinement here (see "Where shaping ends").

The flow

It uses the first and last of Shape Up's four steps (set boundaries, write the pitch). The middle two (find the elements, address rabbit holes) are solution design and belong to refinement (see "Where shaping ends"). The skill writes no code, makes no orders and starts no workers (Shape Up's "two tracks": shaping is separate from building). Shaping is private: a conversation between Jack and the Maestro.

  1. Read. dg clickup get-task. The ticket must be in backlog, or in concept without a pitch. Look at the code and at existing tickets for what already exists.
  2. Set boundaries. Fix the appetite first ("fixed time, variable scope": if the idea does not fit, cut scope, never raise the appetite). Narrow the problem: ask what is really going wrong, and pin it to one specific story that shows why today does not work. Ask the shaper's strategic questions: why does it matter, who is affected, what does success look like, what does it cost compared with the alternatives. Record the baseline: how things get done today without it. A grab-bag ("redesign X", "X 2.0") is reframed or split before shaping. A Big appetite that cannot be narrowed means split. If the problem cannot be pinned down and is not critical, walk away.
  3. Draw the product-level idea. In a few sentences: what the user gets, what is in scope and what is out. Mark what is appealing but not core with a leading ~. No elements, no breadboard, no technical design.
  4. Note the risks. Flag what looks risky or unknown for refinement. Flag only: do not solve it. Propose Sign-off and Mode with one reason each.
  5. Ask. At most three questions to Jack, only on what the draft could not settle. The default three are the book's betting questions: does the problem matter, is the appetite right, is the idea attractive.
  6. Self-review the pitch against the checklist (see "The pitch"), then show it to Jack. On his yes, append it to the ticket description under ## Pitch (the raw idea above it stays untouched), then run dg steward event shaped --ticket <KEY> --by shaping.
  7. Hand over. Tell Jack the next step: "discover it" or "refine it".
Walk away is a valid end. Nothing is committed during shaping ("no conveyor belt"). Shaping can end in "not now". The ticket stays in backlog with a one-line note on why, and no event fires. Jack decides whether to discard it. The skill never discards.

The pitch

  1. Problem: one specific story that shows why the status quo does not work, opening with the baseline (how things get done today without it). The idea is judged against it.
  2. Appetite: Small, Medium or Big. A budget that limits the idea, not an estimate.
  3. Idea: a few sentences at product level: what the user gets. Rough on purpose. Elements marked ~ are nice-to-haves.
  4. Scope in and out, including the no-gos: what is explicitly excluded to fit the appetite.
  5. Risks: what looks risky or unknown, flagged for refinement, not solved.
  6. Sign-off and Mode: proposed values with a reason each (DigiSmith addition; Jack confirms them when he approves into to do).
  7. Open questions: what shaping could not settle. A pitch with open questions can still ship, but they are listed.

Self-review checklist. The pitch must be rough (no element list, no breadboard, no technical design) and bounded (an appetite and no-gos). The skill refuses to show a pitch that has: an idea without a problem; a problem without an idea (unshaped work); no appetite; no scope out; a "TBD" or a field that contradicts another. The book's third property, solved, is not asked of a pitch: solving is refinement's job.

Refinement takes this pitch as its input and does not ask again for problem, scope or appetite.

Where shaping ends

Jack's decision: concept is the what and why at product level (DGS-488). Shape Up's two solution-design steps move to refinement, which is where DGS-488 already puts architecture and solution design.

  • Stays in shaping: boundaries (appetite, narrowed problem), the product-level idea, scope in and out, flagged risks, the pitch.
  • Moves to refinement: find the elements (element list, breadboard, fat marker sketch where layout is the problem) and address rabbit holes (walk the use case in slow motion, question each part that looks solved, then patch, fence off as out of bounds, or cut back). The refinement skill takes the flagged risks as its starting list.
  • Back-edge: if refinement finds the idea does not fit the appetite, the ticket goes back to shaping. It is never stretched to fit (Shape Up's circuit-breaker rule).

The Steward event

shaped moves a ticket from backlog to concept. The skill fires it as its last step, after Jack says the pitch is fine. It needs no quote: it is not one of the two gates (refinement to to do, and acceptance).

  • concept-started is removed. Nothing would fire it any more, and keeping it would let a ticket enter concept without shaping.
  • discovery-started and refinement-started are unchanged and still start from concept.
  • dg steward event stays a state machine: a fixed map of status moves. The change is one row in the rules table and its tests.

Neighbours and old tickets

  • digismith:brainstorming stays the refinement step, narrowed to technical design (see "Decoupled from brainstorming"). Shaping does not reference it.
  • digismith:jira-intake is untouched. It is the Jira door. Shaping is the ClickUp door.
  • Tickets already in concept (DGS-625, 626, 633, 651 and others) stay there. They were moved before any shaping. The first refiner or claimer who touches one runs the skill on it. No mass move.

Decoupled from brainstorming

Jack's decision: shaping has no reference to digismith:brainstorming, and the product-level parts physically leave brainstorming. Each skill is complete on its own. The overlap is resolved by moving the parts, not by sharing them.

The skill list gives each a clear trigger: a backlog ticket (or a concept ticket without a pitch) goes to shaping, a ticket in refinement goes to brainstorming. A refinement ticket without a pitch is sent back to shaping by the Maestro, not by a reference inside a skill.

Shape Up: where we follow it and where we differ

The whole book was read (foreword, chapters 1 to 15, conclusion, three appendices, glossary). Shaping (chapters 2 to 6) is the skill. The rest maps onto DigiSmith like this.

Shape UpDigiSmith
Small team ("small enough to wing it"): no cycles, no formal betting table, but the phases stay: raw idea, shaped pitch, bet, buildJack and the Maestro are that small team. backlog is the raw idea, concept the shaped pitch, Jack's approval into to do the bet, and the factory builds.
Betting table asks: does the problem matter, is the appetite right, is the solution attractive, is it the right time, are the right people freeThe first three are the skill's default questions to Jack (with "solution" read as "idea"). The last two stay with refinement and the factory queue (DGS-670).
No backlogs: ideas not bet on are let go; raw feedback gets a gentle "no"Deliberate difference. ClickUp is the only tracker, so a raw idea stays a backlog ticket. The "gentle no" is the walk-away ending: the ticket stays, with a note.
Circuit breaker: a project that misses its cycle stops and is reshaped, never extended; uphill work at the end means a shaping oversightThe pitch's appetite is the cap. Reshaping on overrun belongs to the failed-ticket decision (DGS-490) and the claim rules (DGS-670). Listed as input to those tickets, not decided here.
Assign projects, not tasks: no upfront task split ("a paper shredder"); the team finds scopes while buildingObservation for the refinement design, not decided here: DGS-488's Refiner splits work into ranked tickets before the build. That is the one place the status model pulls against the book.
Find the elements (breadboard, fat marker) and rabbit holes: shaping steps 2 and 3Deliberate difference (Jack). In DigiSmith these are solution design, so they sit in refinement (DGS-488: architecture and solution design). The shaping skill only flags risks.
Scopes, hill charts, scope hammering, QA for the edges, kickoffBuilding-side practices. Out of scope for the shaping skill. The ~ nice-to-have mark is the one piece taken over, because it helps the pitch.
