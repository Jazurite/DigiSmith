What and why

A ticket is created in backlog as a raw idea. A new skill, digismith:shaping, runs on it. Only after that does the ticket reach concept. So concept now means shaped, no longer being shaped (DGS-488). Claiming, by the factory or by hand, is allowed only after concept (DGS-670).

The method is Basecamp's Shape Up: a pitch with problem, appetite, solution, rabbit holes and no-gos, made in four steps.

The flow

It follows the four Shape Up steps (chapters 3 to 6 of the book). The skill writes no code, makes no orders and starts no workers (Shape Up's "two tracks": shaping is separate from building). Shaping is rough and private: a conversation between Jack and the Maestro.

  1. Read. dg clickup get-task. The ticket must be in backlog, or in concept without a pitch.
  2. Set boundaries. Fix the appetite first ("fixed time, variable scope": if the solution does not fit, cut scope, never raise the appetite). Narrow the problem: ask what is really going wrong, and pin it to one specific story that shows why today does not work. Ask the shaper's strategic questions: why does it matter, who is affected, what does success look like, what does it cost compared with the alternatives. Record the baseline: how things get done today without it. A grab-bag ("redesign X", "X 2.0") is reframed or split before shaping. A Big appetite that cannot be narrowed means split. If the problem cannot be pinned down and is not critical, walk away.
  3. Find the elements. Draft a short list of narrow, specific elements, not a vague phrase. For flows, sketch a breadboard in words: places (a command, a status, a screen), affordances (what can be done there) and connections (what leads where). Use a fat marker sketch only when layout is the core problem. Mark an element that is appealing but not core with a leading ~ (nice-to-have), as in the book, so a later cut is easy. Stay rough: boundaries and rules, not a spec, so the builder keeps room to decide.
  4. Address rabbit holes. Walk the use case in slow motion, start to end. Question each part that looks solved: does it need new technical work, does it assume how parts fit, does it assume a design answer exists? Read the code to check the assumptions. Fix each hole with one of three moves: patch (dictate a simpler answer, written into the pitch), declare out of bounds (a no-go), or cut back (drop a nice-to-have). A hole with no answer goes to Open questions. Then propose Sign-off and Mode with one reason each.
  5. Ask. At most three questions to Jack, only on what the draft could not settle. The default three are the book's betting questions: does the problem matter, is the appetite right, is the solution attractive.
  6. Self-review the pitch against the book's pitfalls (see "The pitch"), then show it to Jack. On his yes, append it to the ticket description under ## Pitch (the raw idea above it stays untouched), then run dg steward event shaped --ticket <KEY> --by shaping.
  7. Hand over. Tell Jack the next step: "discover it" or "refine it".
Walk away is a valid end. Nothing is committed during shaping ("no conveyor belt"). Shaping can end in "not now". The ticket stays in backlog with a one-line note on why, and no event fires. Jack decides whether to discard it. The skill never discards.

The pitch

  1. Problem: one specific story that shows why the status quo does not work, opening with the baseline (how things get done today without it). The solution is judged against it.
  2. Appetite: Small, Medium or Big. A budget that limits the solution, not an estimate.
  3. Solution: the narrow, specific elements, rough: a breadboard in words or a fat marker sketch where it helps, never a wireframe or spec. A note says the builder may find a different way.
  4. Rabbit holes: what could derail the work, each with the call made on it (patch, out of bounds, or cut back). A few lines are enough.
  5. No-gos: what is explicitly out, to fit the appetite.
  6. Sign-off and Mode: proposed values with a reason each (DigiSmith addition; Jack confirms them when he approves into to do).
  7. Open questions: what shaping could not settle. A pitch with open questions can still ship, but they are listed.

Self-review checklist (the book's pitfalls). The pitch must also pass the book's three properties: rough (no wireframe-level detail), solved (every rabbit hole patched, fenced off, or listed as open), bounded (an appetite and no-gos). The skill refuses to show a pitch that has: a solution without a problem; a problem without a solution (unshaped work); no appetite; a wireframe-level spec that boxes the builder in; no rabbit holes or no-gos; a "TBD" or a field that contradicts another.

Refinement reads this pitch as its input, so digismith:brainstorming no longer asks again for problem and scope.

The Steward event

shaped moves a ticket from backlog to concept. The skill fires it as its last step, after Jack says the pitch is fine. It needs no quote: it is not one of the two gates (refinement to to do, and acceptance).

  • concept-started is removed. Nothing would fire it any more, and keeping it would let a ticket enter concept without shaping.
  • discovery-started and refinement-started are unchanged and still start from concept.
  • dg steward event stays a state machine: a fixed map of status moves. The change is one row in the rules table and its tests.

Neighbours and old tickets

  • digismith:brainstorming stays the refinement step, narrowed to technical design (see "Decoupled from brainstorming"). Shaping does not reference it.
  • digismith:jira-intake is untouched. It is the Jira door. Shaping is the ClickUp door.
  • Tickets already in concept (DGS-625, 626, 633, 651 and others) stay there. They were moved before any shaping. The first refiner or claimer who touches one runs the skill on it. No mass move.

Decoupled from brainstorming

Jack's decision: shaping has no reference to digismith:brainstorming, and the product-level parts physically leave brainstorming. Each skill is complete on its own. The overlap is resolved by moving the parts, not by sharing them.

  • Brainstorming is un-vendored. It is removed from VENDORED_SKILLS in scripts/check_vendored_skills.ts and becomes a DigiSmith-owned skill, free to diverge from Superpowers. The upstream drift check no longer covers it.
  • Brainstorming is narrowed. New description: "architecture and solution design for a ticket in refinement". The umbrella trigger ("use before any creative work") is dropped. Its input is the pitch: it does not ask again about purpose, scope, appetite or success criteria. It keeps its name, because init, bootstrap, writing-plans and the DGS-488 text refer to it.
  • No cross-reference either way. Shaping never mentions or invokes brainstorming. The skill list gives each a clear trigger: a backlog ticket (or a concept ticket without a pitch) goes to shaping, a ticket in refinement goes to brainstorming. A refinement ticket without a pitch is sent back to shaping by the Maestro, not by a reference inside a skill.

Shape Up: where we follow it and where we differ

The whole book was read (foreword, chapters 1 to 15, conclusion, three appendices, glossary). Shaping (chapters 2 to 6) is the skill. The rest maps onto DigiSmith like this.

Shape UpDigiSmith
Small team ("small enough to wing it"): no cycles, no formal betting table, but the phases stay: raw idea, shaped pitch, bet, buildJack and the Maestro are that small team. backlog is the raw idea, concept the shaped pitch, Jack's approval into to do the bet, and the factory builds.
Betting table asks: does the problem matter, is the appetite right, is the solution attractive, is it the right time, are the right people freeThe first three are the skill's default questions to Jack. The last two stay with refinement and the factory queue (DGS-670).
No backlogs: ideas not bet on are let go; raw feedback gets a gentle "no"Deliberate difference. ClickUp is the only tracker, so a raw idea stays a backlog ticket. The "gentle no" is the walk-away ending: the ticket stays, with a note.
Circuit breaker: a project that misses its cycle stops and is reshaped, never extended; uphill work at the end means a shaping oversightThe pitch's appetite is the cap. Reshaping on overrun belongs to the failed-ticket decision (DGS-490) and the claim rules (DGS-670). Listed as input to those tickets, not decided here.
Assign projects, not tasks: no upfront task split ("a paper shredder"); the team finds scopes while buildingObservation for the refinement design, not decided here: DGS-488's Refiner splits work into ranked tickets before the build. That is the one place the status model pulls against the book.
Scopes, hill charts, scope hammering, QA for the edges, kickoffBuilding-side practices. Out of scope for the shaping skill. The ~ nice-to-have mark is the one piece taken over, because it helps the pitch.
